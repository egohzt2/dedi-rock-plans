## Why a 1Gbps Port Actually Matters

Let's get the obvious stuff out of the way first. 1 Gbps is 1,000 Mbps, which is ten times the throughput of a 100 Mbps port. Each network frame gets sent ten times faster, so the practical ceiling on simultaneous transfers, concurrent users, and burst traffic all scale up by roughly the same factor.

The less obvious part is what that buys you in real workloads:

- **Streaming and media delivery.** A 1 Gbps dedicated server can sustain multiple high-bitrate video streams or push large media files to a CDN edge without queuing. On a saturated 100 Mbps link, you're constantly choosing between throughput and latency.
- **Game servers.** Latency-sensitive workloads benefit from the headroom — bursts of player traffic during peak hours won't queue behind a bandwidth cap, and patch distributions don't take all night.
- **Backups and large file transfers.** Moving a 500 GB snapshot over 100 Mbps takes the better part of a day. Over a 1 Gbps port that's actually provisioned at line rate, you're looking at a fraction of that.
- **E-commerce and busy web apps.** Concurrent checkout traffic, API calls, and image delivery all live or die by how quickly the server can push bytes out the door.

One thing worth clarifying before you spend money: **1Gbps metered vs. 1Gbps unmetered** are not the same thing. In both cases the port speed is 1 Gbps, but on a metered plan you pay for the bytes you transfer (or get capped after a quota), while on an unmetered plan you're paying for the port itself and can saturate it up to the agreed fair-use threshold. Most "unmetered" offerings still have a fair-use policy to prevent 24/7 line-rate abuse — read the fine print, don't assume "unlimited" means infinite.

DediRock's dedicated servers use a metered model with generous included bandwidth (10 TB up to 40 TB depending on the plan), which is more than enough for the vast majority of real-world workloads and avoids the surprise overage bills that some "unlimited" providers love to bill you for retroactively.

A quick reality check, because this is where most buyers get confused. DediRock's dedicated servers include a metered bandwidth allotment (10 TB to 40 TB depending on the plan) on a 1Gbps port. Which is better? It depends entirely on your traffic pattern:

- If your traffic is bursty and predictable — typical for web apps, e-commerce, most game servers — a metered plan with a generous included quota is almost always cheaper. You're paying for what you use, not for the theoretical ceiling you'll never reach.
